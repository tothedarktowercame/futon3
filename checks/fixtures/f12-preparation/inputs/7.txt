! conclusion: A unit of work is cheaply killed and re-run from pinned inputs, or its preciousness is named as a design decision and paid for knowingly — never resolved incident-by-incident.

  + context: An apparatus runs units of work (frames, slices, jobs) that consume budgets and produce evidence; failures interrupt them mid-flight.

  + IF:
    A unit of work can fail partway, and the question is what to do with its partial state.

  + HOWEVER:
    Re-running feels wasteful (tokens spent) or invalid (the attempt is a datum), so each incident's author preserves the partial state with one more bespoke mechanism — and the sum of those mechanisms becomes most of the apparatus.

  + THEN:
    Decide the tension once, at design time, and say it out loud: which units are replayable (then failures are handled by kill-and-rerun from pinned inputs, marked apparatus-caused so the rerun does not contaminate the measurement) and which are genuinely precious (then the preservation machinery is a costed commitment, not an accident). Where the experiment makes attempts sacred, that sentence belongs in the preregistration.

  + BECAUSE:
    Preciousness is the multiplier on every other failure cost: it is why a quiet failure must be rescued rather than discarded, why blame must be perfect rather than defaulted, and why the failure-class census grows. Replayability is also what lets a review gate afford to refuse work.

    + evidence: APM: the park/latch/rescue inventory, ~57k lines of loop apparatus for a conceptually simple cycle. WM: slices are cheap to kill — the slice-7 review threw out an irreproducible artifact and rebuilt it in-place within the hour, a move impossible when every attempt is evidence.

  + NEXT-STEPS:
    - Classify your units: replayable or precious, in writing, with the experiment's owner signing the precious list.
    - For each precious unit, total the preservation machinery it has bought; that number belongs in the next design review.
